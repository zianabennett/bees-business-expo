# Business Recruitment And Outreach Playbook

## Historical Context And Sample Boundary

At the University of Virginia, I served on the Black Economic Empowerment Society's Finance Committee and later as Finance Chair. I helped recruit businesses for a Black Business Expo through email, in-person outreach, phone calls and flyers. The event featured 30+ Black-owned and student businesses, connecting entrepreneurs with campus buyers and providing visibility and networking opportunities.

This is a new, AI-assisted retrospective portfolio sample based on that experience. It describes how I would structure a future recruiting effort. It is not an original event document, and its procedures are not claims about the historical campaign. No historical response rate, sales, attendance, revenue or channel comparison is available here. The financial and capacity examples come from this repository's separately labeled synthetic exercise.

## Recruitment Objective

Help eligible businesses make an informed participation decision and carry each accepted commitment into event preparation. A useful outreach process answers an owner's practical questions before requesting a commitment: who the event reaches, when participation is required, what the organizer provides, and what the business must bring.

The invitation should explain the opportunity for visibility and direct customer conversations. It should not promise sales, a particular audience size or a financial return without evidence.

## Prepare A Consistent Event Brief

The event lead maintains one brief that every outreach channel uses. Before invitations go out, confirm the event date, location, vendor arrival window, participation fee if any, registration route, available space and participation criteria. Include table and equipment provisions, selling restrictions, accessibility arrangements and the process for requesting power.

If a detail remains unresolved, record its owner and expected decision date. Discuss preliminary interest without presenting an unapproved arrangement as a confirmed offer. Store the current brief with the outreach templates to prevent conflicting answers.

## Give Each Channel A Specific Job

| Channel | Purpose | Recommended Action | Evidence To Keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | Deliver an invitation the owner can review or forward. | State the opportunity, essential logistics and one response route. Personalize using verified business information. | Delivery date, reply, question and next action. |
| In-Person Outreach | Explain the event and hear immediate questions. | Give a short introduction, ask about interest, and agree on a preferred follow-up route. | Business name, stated interest, contact permission and unresolved question. |
| Phone Calls | Resolve a specific question or follow up on an invitation. | Ask whether it is a convenient time, explain the reason for calling and agree on the next step. | Outcome and next action; a call attempt alone is not a conversation. |
| Flyers | Create awareness beyond direct conversations. | Present the event value and one legible response route. Use an approved distribution location. | Distribution location and date; responding businesses can optionally report their source. |

Use the [outreach templates](outreach-templates.md) as draft communication samples. A flyer response may later include an email exchange or phone call. Keep those contacts connected to one business rather than treating them as separate prospects.

## Keep A Useful Outreach Record

Use one record per business with the following fields:

| Field | Reason |
|---|---|
| Business ID And Name | Prevent duplicate records and repeated invitations from different organizers. |
| Business Category | Identify relevant setup questions without assigning an unapproved selection score. |
| Contact Person And Preferred Route | Make follow-up useful and respect the contact preference provided. |
| First Source And Contact History | Distinguish discovery from later assistance across channels. |
| Current Stage | Show whether the business is contacted, interested, confirmed, declined or waitlisted. |
| Last Contact And Next Action Date | Make pending work visible and limit uncoordinated follow-up. |
| Open Question And Owner | Route an issue to someone who can resolve it. |
| Participation Decision | Preserve the explicit acceptance, decline or waitlist response. |
| Setup Handoff | Connect confirmed participation to the readiness record. |

These are proposed fields, not reconstructed historical contact records. Do not put owners' private contact details into a public portfolio repository. The public model can use fictional identifiers while the event team keeps its working list appropriately restricted.

## Move From Interest To Readiness

| Stage | Entry Evidence | Next Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contacted | An invitation was delivered or a conversation occurred. | Record the channel and plan a relevant follow-up. | Outreach Lead |
| Interested | The business asks for details or agrees to continue the conversation. | Resolve questions and request a participation decision. | Outreach Lead |
| Confirmed | The business accepts the terms and the event has verified a place. | Collect requirements and open the logistics handoff. | Event Lead |
| Ready | Required setup information, a unique booth, arrival arrangements and necessary power approval are complete. | Send final instructions and track changes. | Logistics Lead |

Declined and waitlisted are separate participation statuses. A declined business should leave the active follow-up queue. A waitlist message must explain that a place is unconfirmed and give the next update date. Readiness is assessed only after confirmation; it does not replace the participation decision.

## Use A Bounded Follow-Up Cadence

The following is a proposed planning cadence aligned to the existing [run of show](run-of-show.md). T0 means event day; these relative deadlines are illustrative.

| Timing | Review |
|---|---|
| T−21 | Confirm the event brief, available places and financial limits. |
| T−14 | Review invitation responses and assign unresolved questions. |
| T−10 | Request setup details from confirmed businesses; update interest and waitlist records. |
| T−8 | Review participation decisions against available places before additional commitments. |
| T−6 | Hand outstanding readiness issues to the relevant operations owner. |
| T−2 | Send arrival instructions and confirm how vendors can reach the event team. |

For an unanswered invitation, make one relevant follow-up after several business days, then a final closure message before the decision deadline. Adjust when an owner requests a different date. Repeated contact through every channel is not a substitute for a response. Honor an explicit decline immediately.

## Connect Recruitment To Capacity And Finance

The synthetic dataset has fourteen places and twelve confirmed businesses, leaving two places for three open invitations. Confirming all three would create one excess commitment. The event lead should verify additional capacity or use a communicated waitlist before issuing that final confirmation. The model contains no agreed selection criteria and cannot decide which business should lose a place.

Resource questions also belong in the handoff. A business requesting power may need a logistics decision and a cost review. In the synthetic budget, $515 paid, $560 committed but unpaid and $160 in remaining estimates produce a $1,235 forecast. The $1,350 ceiling leaves $115 available. An additional $80 for power fits within that ceiling; adding a separate $50 supplies request as well would exceed it by $15.

Those calculations establish a constraint, not automatic spending approval. Check whether the support is necessary, whether its cost is already included, and whether an offset or additional funding is verified. See the [decision analysis](decision-analysis.md) for the existing model's assumptions and alternatives.

## Measure The Recruiting Process Honestly

| Measure | Definition | Interpretation Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Direct Outreach Response Rate | Unique directly invited businesses that reply divided by unique businesses receiving a direct invitation, with a defined observation window. | An unsuccessful delivery is tracked separately; flyer exposure is not a known denominator. |
| Confirmed Participation Rate | Confirmed businesses from the direct-outreach group divided by unique businesses receiving a direct invitation in that group and window. | Capacity, eligibility and event terms can affect the rate. |
| Follow-Up Age | Elapsed time since the next action became due. | A longer interval may reflect an owner-requested date; preserve that context. |
| Unresolved Questions | Open participation questions grouped by responsible owner. | Count each question once and retain its business link. |
| Readiness Rate | Ready businesses divided by confirmed businesses. | This measures preparation, not attendance or commercial success. |

Record a first source and later contacts separately. A business discovered through a flyer and confirmed after a phone conversation demonstrates multiple contacts; it does not prove which channel caused the decision. Review channel patterns only when the underlying records and denominators support a comparison.

After the event, participation attendance and vendor feedback could help assess whether the opportunity met expectations. Sales or revenue should be reported only when actually collected with a clear method. This sample does not invent those outcomes.
