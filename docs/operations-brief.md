# Operating brief

## Purpose and decision window

At T-6, the organizing team needs a dependable answer to two questions: which confirmed vendors can participate under the current plan, and what must happen to prepare the rest within the available budget? T denotes the event day. The scenario and its relationship to the original experience are defined in the [case study](../README.md).

The operating scope includes participation status, preparation dependencies, booth capacity, and expense forecasting. It excludes vendor selection scoring, attendee demand, sales performance, and claims about the original event's results.

## Readiness definition

A vendor counts as ready only when participation is confirmed and every applicable preparation requirement is complete:

1. Setup information has been received.
2. Any requested power arrangement has been approved.
3. A booth in the inventory is assigned to that vendor alone.
4. The vendor's arrival window is confirmed.

A vendor who does not request power does not need a power approval. An invitation alone does not enter the readiness denominator. The calculation is ready confirmed vendors divided by all confirmed vendors. If none are confirmed, the report displays no percentage rather than dividing by zero or implying full readiness.

## Dependencies and next actions

The action queue shows one next step per unresolved vendor. Missing setup information comes first because the team needs those requirements before completing placement. Power approval follows, then a conflicting or missing booth assignment, then arrival confirmation. The report still displays every remaining gap; it does not conceal later work when it chooses the first action.

P1 includes a selected blocker that is due or overdue, an unresolved power request, a duplicate booth assignment, or an event-level capacity issue. P2 covers other confirmed-vendor preparation work. P3 covers open invitations. Within a priority, more overdue items come first, followed by the earlier deadline and vendor ID. This policy is explicit and reviewable; it is not a prediction of the financial value of each vendor.

## Responsibilities

| Role in the model | Responsibility | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor coordinator | Obtain setup details and arrival confirmations | Updated fields and a recorded confirmation |
| Logistics lead | Resolve power requests and booth assignments | Approved arrangement and a unique compatible booth |
| Outreach lead | Close outstanding invitation decisions | Participation status recorded after capacity review |
| Event lead | Control total commitments and resolve competing demands | Confirmed count stays within available places |
| Finance lead | Review category variances and prospective cost increases | Updated forecast and documented spending decision |

## Capacity control

The inventory has 14 places. Twelve confirmed vendors consume 12 places even though two lack an assignment. The three open invitations therefore create an exposure of one place if all become commitments. Nine unique booths are currently assigned because two confirmed vendors share B06. Neither unassigned records nor duplicated assignments create additional capacity.

The model raises a capacity review instead of automatically declining a vendor. The event lead must decide how to handle invitations using an appropriate, consistently applied participation policy.

## Financial control

Forecast spending equals paid costs plus committed but unpaid costs plus remaining estimates. Each cost belongs to one state. Paying an existing commitment moves its amount from committed to paid without increasing the forecast.

Category plans total $1,200, with a separate $150 contingency allowance. Contingency increases the spending ceiling; it is not a fictional expense. The baseline forecast uses $35 of that allowance. The additional-exposure scenario crosses the ceiling, creating a concrete decision before new spending is authorized.
